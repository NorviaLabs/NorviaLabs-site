What is Norvia Labs

An open-source lab for the agentic era

AI agents are becoming active participants in software development. The tooling underneath them needs better interfaces for execution, permissions, observability, contracts, provenance, auditability, and human oversight.

Norvia Labs publishes focused experiments on that layer, MIT licensed and built in the open. Forge is the active project — a terminal-native AI coding environment. Guard, Contract, Route, and Audit are queued experiments exploring governance, contracts, routing, and auditability for agentic systems.

How we build

The same principles run through every experiment

1

Public by default

Experiments start open. Source, issues, and notes ship together so others can reproduce, fork, or kill the idea honestly.

2

One sharp idea each

Small products that do one thing well and compose. No kitchen-sink frameworks — just a clear README and a hard boundary.

3

Measure what matters

Benchmarks, metrics, and cost are first-class. If we cannot measure it, it is not ready to recommend.

4

MIT, no lock-in

Experiments are MIT licensed when they ship. No accounts, no telemetry tax, no closed tiers — the library or CLI is the whole thing.

Follow the work in the open

Forge is shipping now and MIT licensed. The rest of the lab — governance, contracts, routing, and audit — is queued in public.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is Norvia Labs?
Norvia Labs is an open-source lab exploring developer tools and infrastructure for agentic software engineering. The active project is Forge, a terminal-native AI coding environment. Additional experiments explore governance, contracts, routing, and audit around agentic systems.
Is everything open source?
Yes. Experiments are MIT licensed as they ship. There are no closed product tiers — when a project ships, the repository on GitHub is the whole thing.
How do I choose an experiment?
Start from the problem. Use the “Find a tool” decision table on this page, or browse the portfolio. Active work is shipping now; Queue experiments are listed but not yet interactive.
Can I contribute?
Absolutely. Use the contact form or open an issue on GitHub. Small, well-scoped changes and reproducible bug reports are especially welcome.
Who is behind Norvia Labs?
Norvia Labs is built by Mohit Ranka. The lab is separate from commercial work; embedded engineering leadership engagements live at mohitranka.com.
How is this different from a product company site?
Norvia Labs is a lab, not a SaaS catalog. Experiments may graduate, pivot, or be archived in public. The goal is useful software and honest learning — not a sales funnel.
